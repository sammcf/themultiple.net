Design system

One plane. Three plateaus. One record.

A durable visual system for themultiple.net — a shared graphite substrate that never changes, one spot ink per app, and a single component that makes three unrelated products visibly one family.

ThesisWhy this

They are all record-keepers.

Collage keeps an immutable pipeline of edit steps. Steward's core invariant is that a commitment must end in a recorded outcome, with provenance. Ficta separates Claim from Fact and keeps a revisable history of how a theory was reached.

Three products with nothing in common at the category level, and one structure in common at the model level: an ordered chain that closes. That is what the system is built on. Everything else is quiet around it.

The name carries two readings and the system uses both. From Deleuze and Guattari, a multiplicity has no central term — so the index has no hero product and no ranking, just a plane of equal entries. From printing, a multiple is an edition — same plates, same stock, different ink per impression.

The planeSubstrate

Graphite, ruled with hairlines.

Dark is canonical; light is derived from the same token names. The neutrals carry a slight cool bias so they read as chosen rather than inherited — and pointedly not cream, which is where both of the current pages ended up.

MarkWordmark · monogram

the Multiple, never The Multiple.

Lowercase article, everywhere — including at the start of a sentence and in the browser title bar. The system already demotes the parent: the plane carries no ink of its own and the index refuses to rank its entries. Lowercasing the article is that same move applied to the name. It is an article, not a title.

The treatment then follows a rule the system already has. Plex Mono carries labels and metadata everywhere else on the site, and grammatically an article is metadata about a noun — so the is set in mono, and Multiple in Bricolage at full weight. The wordmark obeys an existing rule instead of inventing a special case, which is the thing that makes a wordmark survivable.

theMultiple A · mono article — recommended
theMultiple B · all Bricolage, article recedes
theMultiple C · stacked masthead

A is the working default and the one in this page's masthead. C is worth keeping in reserve for a favicon-adjacent or vertical context — it is the same two parts, just re-broken.

The monogram comes back

theMultiple

A capital sigma entangled with a lowercase phi is already saying what the wordmark says, which is why it should come back rather than be replaced.

Same shapeA subordinate lowercase form entangled with a dominant capital. Σφ and the Multiple are the same structure twice — once in Greek, once in English. Nobody needs it explained; it just makes the two marks feel like one system.
φ is the pointGolden ratio, Euler's totient, magnetic flux, phase angle, potential, work function, the wave function. One glyph whose meaning is set entirely by which field you happen to be standing in. There is no better single character for a collection of unrelated tools that share a plane and nothing else.
Σ sumsΠ is the product operator; Σ is the sum — and sum is the stronger reading. The apps add up to a body of work; they do not compose into a larger product. No dependency, no hierarchy, no roadmap where one unlocks another.
Neither winsIn the drawing the sigma's upper bar passes in front of the phi's stem, and the phi's stem passes in front of the sigma's lower bar. A weave, not a stack. The refusal of a central term, at 78 by 100 units.
MastheadLogotype. Monogram only if the header is taller than one line.
Favicon · avatarMonogram alone. It is the only form that survives 32px.
ColophonLogotype, in the impression block.
NeverThe two locked side by side below 2rem. At that size the mark is one or the other.

The monogram is drawn as four SVG paths rather than set as text, which is deliberate: the shipped woff2 files are latin-subset and carry no Greek. Anything that renders Σ or φ as live text needs a Greek subset added, so keep the mark as geometry and the problem never arises.

SignatureThe record strip

One component. Three truths.

Every app page carries a strip of that product's actual chain, taken from its own specification. The order encodes something real, so the sequence is information rather than decoration — and the terminal node is drawn twice over, because in all three products the record closes.

The strip draws itself left to right as it enters the viewport — one @property number animated on the list by a scroll timeline, which the nodes read to stagger themselves. No JavaScript. Where animation-timeline is unsupported it renders fully drawn, and prefers-reduced-motion switches it off. Below 38rem it rotates to vertical, where it stops being a pipeline and becomes a ledger. This is the only motion in the system.

On the name

Ficta. In music before roughly 1600, sources routinely omit accidentals the performer is expected to supply. Musica recta is the given gamut — what the source actually says. Musica ficta is what informed judgement supplies on top of it. Editors print ficta above the staff, never inline, so a reader can always separate the source from the inference. Change one ficta decision and the consequences propagate through the passage, which is why musicologists argue it as a constraint problem rather than a matter of taste.

That is the domain model in a single medieval term: Fact and Claim, Judgement, provenance, and a reading that stays revisable because the reasoning was never thrown away. Truth maintenance, four centuries early.

It also disposes of Urtext, the otherwise obvious musical candidate. An Urtext edition is arborescent — it hunts the one originary text that every variant descends from, a root with filiation. Ficta is rhizomatic: the source underdetermines, readings multiply laterally, any reading connects to any other, and the work is to keep the justifications alive rather than collapse them into an origin. The engine maintains truth precisely by refusing a single root.

The objection that it sounds like fiction inverts on inspection. Ficta means feigned, and the entire discipline exists so that a feigned note can never quietly pass itself off as a sourced one.

Runners-up were Recta (names the Fact side, undersells the inference), Emendation (active, but shared with textual scholarship) and Concordance (musicologically exact, dry). Ossia was the one worth mourning — an ossia is a variant reading printed on a small staff above the main one — but it belongs to ossia score / libossia. Neume is taken too. Ficta came up clear; run the domain, crates.io and npm before you commit.
